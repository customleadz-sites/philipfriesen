What this means

Google demand is real but small — and it's regional. ≈290 searches a month covers the whole Paris area; most of those people are 20+ miles away with washes of their own. Google alone will not fill the bays.
A small Google campaign is still worth running. Nobody else is bidding, clicks cost about $1, and 85% of searchers want a wash right now. $100–150/month tightly targeted around Honey Grove captures essentially everything worth buying. Do not spend more than that here.
The bigger Google win is free: the Business Profile. Those ≈290 monthly searches mostly land on Google Maps. Finish the Buzz & Buff rename, load photos, build on the 5-star start, and track "directions" taps. That's where "car wash near me" actually gets decided.
Put the rest of the budget into Facebook. The people who need to hear "under new management, $3 wash, credit cards accepted" are locals who never search Google — you reach them on Facebook. The ad I'd run first is the video we talked about: you walking up to the wash with a car getting washed behind you. I'll write the script — you just film it on your phone. That one video works as the paid ad and the pinned post, and it does the one job that matters most: telling the town this isn't the old run-down wash anymore.
Wait for the new sign before turning anything on. Ads that say Buzz & Buff pointing at a wash labeled Main Street break trust. Rebrand first, advertise second.

How we corrected the numbers. Google's raw export claimed far more search volume than actually exists, so every figure above went through four honesty passes: (1) we removed 404 phrases that don't apply — competitor brand names (the biggest, a chain 210 searches/mo), automatic and touchless washes, detailing services, pressure-washer products, and "buy a car wash business" searches; (2) phrases Google reports at "10 searches/month" are rounded up — we count each as 3; (3) Google counts the same search under many similar phrases, so we merged 19 duplicate groups (e.g. "car wash near me" was being counted 16 ways); (4) a further 30% discount on the total, because Google's own documentation says volumes overlap and independent studies find Keyword Planner overestimates. No number here double-counts, and nothing is inflated to make ads look better than they are.
